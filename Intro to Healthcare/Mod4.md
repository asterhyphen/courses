## 1. Intermediaries Overview

### *Definition:*

- Intermediaries are also called **plans, payers, and insurers**.
- Essential to the functioning of the healthcare system.
- Provide mechanisms for **risk pooling** by taking on risk from individuals.
- Handle payments to providers.
- Define coverage rules that influence how patients use care.

### *Types:*

- **Public intermediaries:** Run by or under the auspices of a government.
- **Private intermediaries:** Operate as private businesses.

---

# 2. Groups Intermediaries Work With

Intermediaries work with **three groups**:

### 1. *Providers*

- Establish arrangements with healthcare providers.
- Create structures for caring for covered patients.
- Determine how providers get paid.

### 2. *Patients*

- Establish the terms under which healthcare is covered.
- Influence:
  - What services patients can receive.
  - How easily patients can access care.

### 3. *Purchasers*

- People who pay for coverage.
- Customers or buyers of the intermediary.

### *Main Goals:*

- Maintain relationships with patients and providers.
- Keep people satisfied with their care.
- Keep costs under control.
- Provide high-quality healthcare.
- Enable access to care.

### *Broader Forces:*

- High and rising healthcare costs.
- Need to provide high-quality healthcare.
- Need to enable access to care.

---

# 3. Intermediary Tools for Influencing Healthcare

Intermediaries use **5 major tools**:

1. Management of provider availability and networks.
2. Provider payment methods and levels.
3. Patient cost sharing.
4. Utilization review, gatekeepers, and other methods.
5. Coverage decisions.

---

# 4. Provider Availability & Networks

### *Network / Provider Panel:*

- Set of providers organized by an intermediary to care for enrolled patients.

### *Selective Contracting:*

- Intermediary selects **some but not all** available providers for its network.

### *Provider Selection Criteria:*

- Quality
- Efficiency
- Payments required
- Location

### *Types of Panels:*

#### **Closed Panel**

- Enrollees are required to see providers in the panel.
- The intermediary may not contribute to costs if patients go outside the network.

#### **Open Panel**

- Members can generally choose **any provider**.

#### **Semi-open / Semi-closed Panel**

- Network is established.
- Members are encouraged to use network providers through favorable cost-sharing.
- Some costs may still be covered for out-of-network providers.

---

# 5. Provider Payment Methods & Levels

Intermediaries use payment structures to influence **care utilization and costs**.

### *Payment Methods for Physicians & Professionals:*

- Fee-for-service
- Capitation
- Episode-based payment

### *Payment Methods for Hospitals:*

- Fee-for-service
- Per diem
- DRG
- Global budget

### *Two Dimensions of Payment:*

#### 1. **Form of Payment**

Determines **how** providers are paid.

#### 2. **Level of Payment**

Determines **how much** providers are paid.

- Fee schedules and DRG payment amounts can be set higher or lower.

### *Selective Contracting + Payment:*

- Selective contracting can strengthen an intermediary's **bargaining ability** when negotiating with providers.

---

# 6. Patient Cost Sharing

### *Definition:*

- Money paid by patients to providers when they receive care.

### *Types:*

#### **Deductible**

- Amount a patient pays out of pocket **before** the intermediary starts contributing to healthcare costs.

#### **Copayment**

- Payment a patient makes **each time** they see a provider.

#### **Coinsurance**

- **Percentage of the bill** that the patient is responsible for.

### *Cost-Sharing Variations:*

Can vary by:

- Type of service.
- Level of spending.
- Out-of-pocket limit/maximum.
- Network status.
- Provider tier.

### *Goal:*

- Encourage patients to make more efficient and thoughtful choices about the healthcare they need.

---

# 7. Utilization Review & Gatekeepers

These are tools used to **directly control or manage healthcare utilization**.

## 1. Gatekeeper Requirement

- Patient must select or be assigned to a **primary care physician**.
- PCP becomes the first point of contact for medical issues.
- A referral may be required before:
  - Seeing a specialist.
  - Getting testing.
  - Receiving certain services.

---

## 2. Utilization Review

### *Definition:*

- Plan efforts to monitor patient utilization and influence decisions about care.

### *Three Main Types:*

#### **A. Pre-authorization / Prior Review**

- Plan must approve certain services **before** they are provided.
- May require the patient to try a **less expensive therapy first**.
- Can target potentially overused, expensive, low-value services.

#### **B. Concurrent Review**

- Care is monitored **while it is being provided**.
- Common for hospital stays.
- Insurance may contact the hospital if it believes the patient has stayed too long.
- Hospital must justify why the patient should remain.

#### **C. Retrospective Review**

- Care is reviewed **after it has been completed**.
- Plan evaluates whether the care was appropriate.

### *Other Methods:*

- Monitor provider utilization data.
- Monitor quality scores.
- Provide feedback to providers.
- Send reminders and information to patients.

---

# 8. Coverage Decisions

### *Definition:*

- Intermediaries influence healthcare use and costs by deciding **which services they will cover**.

### *Medical Necessity:*

- Plans generally try to cover services considered **medically necessary**.
- These are services medical professionals commonly consider warranted or indicated.

### *Coverage Decision Criteria:*

- **Efficacy**
- **Cost**
- **Cost-effectiveness**

### *Example:*

- New drugs and expensive treatments can create difficult **cost-effectiveness questions**.

---

# 9. Combining Intermediary Tools

Intermediaries need to:

- Determine specific goals.
- Determine which providers and people they work with.
- Deal and compete with other intermediaries.
- Design approaches to meet their goals.
- Mix and match existing tools.
- Create new tools when needed.
- Adapt over time.

### *Intermediaries Can Differ By:*

- The combination of tools they use.
- How extensively they use each tool.

---

# 10. Common Health Plan Designs

## 1. Traditional / Indemnity Insurance

- Minimal use of intermediary tools.
- **Open panel**
- **Fee-for-service**
- **No gatekeepers**
- Limited utilization review.
- Often higher patient cost sharing.

---

## 2. HMO

### *HMO = Health Maintenance Organization*

- Focuses particularly on **prevention and maintenance of health**.
- In many ways, the opposite of traditional indemnity insurance.

### *Characteristics:*

- Defined network.
- **Closed panel**
- Stronger provider payment incentives.
- Gatekeepers common.
- Utilization review common.
- Often less patient cost sharing.

---

## 3. PPO

### *PPO = Preferred Provider Organization*

- Falls between traditional indemnity insurance and HMOs.

### *Characteristics:*

- Semi-open / semi-closed panel.
- Moderate provider payment incentives.
- Gatekeeper uncommon.
- Some patient cost sharing.

### *Managed Care:*

- Plan designs where the intermediary makes efforts to manage care using intermediary tools.
- **HMOs and PPOs are examples of managed care plans.**

---

# 11. Newer Health Plan Designs

## 1. High Deductible Plan

- Common structure is similar to a **PPO**.
- Has a **higher deductible**.
- Has **lower insurance premiums**.

## 2. Narrow Network Plan

- Similar to an HMO or PPO.
- Uses a **smaller, more selected provider network**.

---

# 12. Private Insurance Companies

- Most common source of insurance for people **under age 65 in the U.S.**
- Insurance companies may be:
  - Large and national.
  - Smaller and regional.
- Subject to government oversight.

### *Products Offered:*

- HMOs
- PPOs
- High deductible plans
- Other plan types

### *Common Coverage:*

- Doctors
- Hospitals
- Testing
- Prescription drugs
- Other healthcare services

### *Commonly Excluded:*

- Long-term care
- Optometric/eye care
- Dental care

These may require **separate coverage**.

### *How Private Insurance is Obtained:*

#### **Employer-Sponsored**

- Provided by an employer.

#### **Individual Market**

- Purchased directly by individuals.
- Marketplaces are established in each state.
- Government subsidies may help lower-income people purchase coverage.

---

# 13. Medicare

### *Definition:*

- Large **public intermediary**.

### *Covers:*

- People **over age 65**.
- Permanently disabled people.
- People with **end-stage renal disease**.

### *Financing:*

- Largely financed through taxes.
- Some premiums are paid by enrollees.

### *Administration:*

- Run by the **Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS)**.

---

## Medicare Plan Options

### 1. Traditional Medicare — Parts A & B

- Traditional indemnity coverage.
- **Open panel**
- Little utilization review.
- No gatekeepers.
- Doctors paid using a **fee schedule**.
- Hospitals paid using a **DRG system**.
- High cost sharing.

### 2. Medicare Advantage — Part C

- Medicare contracts with **private insurance companies** to take Medicare enrollees.
- Plans are often:
  - HMO
  - PPO
- Strong focus on the Medicare market.

### 3. Medicare Part D

- Prescription drug coverage.
- Purchased as an add-on to traditional Medicare.
- Often incorporated into Medicare Advantage plans.

### *Medicare Supplement / Medigap:*

- Supplemental insurance obtained by traditional Medicare enrollees.
- Helps cover:
  - Cost sharing.
  - Other things not covered by Medicare.

### *Medicare Innovation:*

- Pioneer in:
  - Fee schedules.
  - DRGs.
  - ACO models.
- Medicare Advantage brings newer plan designs.

---

# 14. Medicaid

### *Definition:*

- Large **public intermediary**.
- Covers **lower-income populations**.
- Overseen by:
  - Federal government.
  - State governments.
- Largely financed through **tax revenue**.

### *Two Main Forms:*

#### 1. **Traditional Medicaid**

- Original form.
- Generally traditional indemnity-style coverage.

#### 2. **Medicaid Managed Care**

- More commonly found.
- Uses HMOs or other managed-care plans for Medicaid recipients.

### *Challenges:*

- Limited funding.
- Limited payments to providers caring for Medicaid patients.
- Eligibility limits vary from state to state.
- Ability to cover low-income populations differs across states.
- Raising sufficient funds can be difficult in complex fiscal and political environments.

# 15. Lessons for AI & Data in Healthcare

Intermediaries can use AI and data to help with:

### *Provider Management*

- Determine which providers to contract with.
- Determine which providers to drop from networks.
- Provide feedback for designing payment approaches.

### *Care Management*

- Identify complex medical-care enrollees.
- Determine which pre-authorization requests should be approved.
- Identify hospital stays that may be too long.

### *Cost Sharing*

- Determine optimal cost-sharing structures.

### *Marketing*

- Find likely customers.
- Market insurance products to them.

### *Claims Processing*

- Process large numbers of medical claims.
- Determine which claims to pay.
- Determine which claims not to pay.
- Identify errors.
- Identify potential fraud.
