## 1. Overview of AI Applications in Healthcare

Modern healthcare systems contain many organizations that collect and analyze large amounts of data.

### Major Data Sources

- Healthcare providers
- Health insurance companies
- Government programs such as:
  - Medicare
  - Medicaid

### Potential Applications of AI

- Increasing the accuracy of diagnosis.
- Robotic surgeries.
- Identifying candidates for drug development.
- Determining the best treatments for specific patients.

### Key Ethical Questions

AI developers, users, patients, and other stakeholders need to consider:

1. **Do AI tools help or harm?**
   - Patients
   - Families
   - Caregivers
   - Healthcare providers

2. **Are AI tools socially just or not?**

Ethical issues can arise from:

- The nature of the data used.
- How data are collected.
- How AI models are designed.
- How AI outputs are interpreted and used.

### Major Concern

**Privacy and security of digital health data**

- Healthcare data are particularly sensitive.
- Protecting health data is therefore a major ethical concern.

---

# 2. Bias & Systematic Error in AI

AI can produce biased conclusions when models are trained on data that do not adequately represent the populations or features of interest.

### Systematic Error

- AI models may contain systematic errors because of problems in the training data.
- This is especially important in healthcare because AI outputs can affect:
  - Medical decisions.
  - Treatment decisions.
  - Life-and-death decisions.

### Potentially Affected Groups

Systematic errors can result in unfair decisions, particularly for socially disadvantaged groups such as:

- Racial minorities
- Women
- Children
- People with low incomes

---

# 3. Transparency & Regulation

### Lack of Transparency

- Some AI models are difficult to understand or interpret.
- Lack of transparency is an important ethical concern.

### Lack of Standards

- There are currently few standards or regulations for evaluating the safety and effectiveness of many AI-based healthcare products.

### Responsibility of Clinicians & Health Systems

Clinicians and health systems using AI for healthcare decisions need to understand:

- Limitations of AI techniques.
- Limitations of the data.
- Limitations of the models.
- How these limitations apply to their specific patient populations.

---

# 4. Conflicting & Competing Interests

Conflicting or competing interests are particularly important in healthcare.

### Financial Interests

- Clinicians and healthcare organizations try to serve the best interests of patients.
- However, they also have interests of their own.
- Financial interests are especially important in healthcare delivery.

Understanding these competing interests helps explain how AI models can:

- Align with healthcare incentives.
- Become misaligned with healthcare incentives.
- Affect patient care and well-being.

# 5. Ethical Frameworks for Healthcare & AI

Healthcare has ethical, legal, and regulatory factors that influence how care is financed and delivered.

### 1. Professional Ethics

- Professionals are ethically bound by professional ethics.
- Professional ethics are expressed through:
  - Codes
  - Guidelines

### Professionals

Professionals are people entrusted with the well-being of individuals who seek their specialized expertise during times of need.

### 2. Social Contract

Physicians are allowed access to:

- Private health information.
- Financial information.

They may also perform actions that would be considered criminal in other contexts.

This is permitted because of the social contract associated with professions when such actions are necessary to serve the well-being of patients.

### 3. Self-Regulation

- A key feature of professions is that professionals are given significant ability to self-regulate.

### 4. Fiduciary Duties

Professionals have **fiduciary duties**.

### Fiduciary Duty

An ethical obligation to serve the **best interests of clients**.

- Healthcare systems.
- Providers.
- Insurers.

## 2. Medical Risk

The probability of medical harm associated with the use of healthcare.

# 12. Example: AI Predicting High-Cost Patients

An insurance company may build an AI model using:

- Insurance claims.
- Electronic health records.
- Consumer data.

### Goal

Predict which members are likely to incur the highest healthcare costs over the next year.

### Ethical Challenge

Vulnerable populations may have disproportionately high healthcare costs, including:

- Poor people.
- People with disabilities.
- Racial and ethnic minorities.

### Important Requirement

To reduce discrimination against vulnerable groups, predictive models need to **risk adjust accurately**.

---

# 13. Proxy Measures

### Problem

Healthcare data often do not directly measure the thing we actually want to measure.

Therefore, models may use **proxy measures**.

### Proxy Measure

A measurement used as an indirect representation of the actual outcome or feature of interest.

### Problem With Proxy Measures

Proxies are imperfect.

This creates the possibility of:

**Proxy Measures → Systematic Error / Bias → Discrimination**

### Discrimination

Bias that leads to negative consequences for certain groups.

### Important Point

When available data cannot adequately explain the outcome of interest, the model's limitations must be recognized.

### Avoiding Ethical Pitfalls

Model designers need:

- Deep knowledge of clinical characteristics of patients in the dataset.
- Familiarity with limitations of available data.

# 14. AI & Hospital Readmissions

## Example

A large information technology company provides analytics to health systems.

The service integrates:

- Patient health data.
- Insurance claims data.

### Goal

Help reduce:

- Inpatient admissions.
- Emergency department admissions.
- Readmissions.

### 30-Day Readmission Prediction

The model predicts whether a patient will be readmitted to a hospital within **30 days of discharge**.

---

# 15. Medicare Hospital Readmissions Reduction Program (HRRP)

### HRRP

The **Hospital Readmissions Reduction Program** creates financial incentives to reduce excessive hospital readmissions.

### How It Works

- Hospitals can receive fines when they have excess readmissions within 30 days.
- Readmissions are adjusted for medical risks.

### Purpose

Create financial incentives to reduce avoidable readmissions.

### Ethical Concern

There is evidence that fines reduce readmissions.

However, there is also evidence that the way admissions are reduced can potentially result in:

- Poorer care.
- Inappropriate care.
- No care at all.

### Key Point

The ethical impact depends not only on the AI prediction but also on **how the model's information is used in practice**.

---

# 16. AI & Bundled Payments

Another example involves predicting costs for patients receiving joint replacements.

### Purpose

The model may be developed in response to incentives designed to encourage:

**Value-based care**

### Bundled Payments

Bundled payments focus on lowering healthcare costs by paying multiple providers for an **episode of care**.

### How Bundled Payments Work

- Payment is bundled for all care associated with an episode.
- Providers manage the costs as a group.

### If Costs Exceed Payment

- Providers share the financial burden.

### If Costs Are Lower Than Payment

- Providers share what remains.

### Purpose of Bundled Payments

- Encourage better coordination of care.
- Improve patient outcomes during transitions between healthcare settings.
- Align incentives among providers across different healthcare settings.

---

# 17. Bundled Payments & Health Disparities

Existing health disparities can interact with bundled payment programs.

### Risk

If predictive models:

- Do not include the right data.
- Do not risk-adjust appropriately.

Then health disparities may be **reinforced or worsened**.

### Key Relationship

**Poor Data → Poor Risk Adjustment → Biased Prediction → Potentially Greater Health Disparities**

---

# 18. Main Ethical Takeaways

### 1. Healthcare Systems Use Incentives

Healthcare systems deliberately use incentives to influence behavior.

### 2. Incentives Create a Need for Prediction

Because incentives involve:

- Costs.
- Quality.
- Utilization.
- Risk.

There is a need to predict patient and healthcare risks.

### 3. AI Can Improve Prediction

AI can improve the accuracy of:

- Classification.
- Prediction.
- Risk identification.

### 4. Incentives Can Have Unintended Consequences

An incentive designed to improve healthcare can potentially produce unexpected effects.

### 5. Proxy Measures Can Create Bias

It is important to understand:

- Proxy measures.
- Limits of healthcare data.
- Potential bias in models.

### 6. Model Outputs Can Change Behavior

It is important to understand how people using AI outputs may behave and whether their behavior could create unintended consequences.
