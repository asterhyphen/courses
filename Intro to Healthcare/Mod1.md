# Roles 
***Physician:*** Has degree and is referred by their specialty (e.g., Dermatologist).
- **Outpatient:** Clinic.
- **Inpatient:** inside hospital.

***Nurse:*** Provides direct patient care and administers medications, and assists physicians.

***Nurse Practitioner:*** Can diagnose illnesses, prescribe medications, and work independently.

***Physician Assistant:*** Licensed healthcare provider working under physician supervision. Diagnoses, treats patients, prescribes medications and assists in surgeries.

---
# Organizations

***Physician Practice (Clinic):*** Outpatient, owned by one or more physicians.

***Hospital:*** Large healthcare organization providing outpatient and inpatient with ICUs and all.

***Independent Organization:*** Not directly hospital owned like labs, imaging centers etc.

---
# Levels of Care
***Primary Care:*** Generalised first stop where assistants or nurses come in (outpatient).

***Secondary Care:*** Specialist comes in or consultants (outpatient and inpatient).

***Tertiary Care:*** Referral based highly specialised physician like surgeries (mostly inpatient).

***Quaternary Care:*** Expiremental treatments and all.

## Key Challenges in Healthcare

## 1. High & Rising Costs
- Healthcare spending is often measured as **Healthcare Spending / GDP**
- Reasons healthcare costs continue to rise:
  - Aging population
  - Higher income and living standards
  - Increase in prices
  - Better technology → more healthcare utilization

## 2. Quality Challenges
***Underuse:*** Patients do **not receive** services that they should receive.
- Example: Missing recommended vaccinations or screenings.

***Overuse:*** Providing services that add **little or no value** and can also cause overdose or harm.

***Misuse:*** Wrong treatment, wrong medication, or treatment used when it shouldn't be.

## 3. Access Challenges
- Lack of insurance coverage.
- Cultural barriers.
- Language barriers.
- Shortage of healthcare providers.

# Healthcare Data Sources

***Provider Data:*** Electronic Health Records (**EHR**). Used for patient care and is highly regulated due to privacy concerns.

***Intermediary Data:*** Claims and payment records. Shows services received and healthcare costs.

***Government Data:*** Tracks regulated healthcare services. Examples: Hospital & Physician licenses.

***Pharmacy Data:***  Medications sold, useful for medication history.
