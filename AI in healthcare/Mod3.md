# Module 3 — Hospitals, Other Provider Organizations & Related Payment Systems

## 1. Hospital Overview

### *Definition:*

- Organizations that provide **facilities and staff** to offer medical care.
- Can be:
  - For-profit
  - Not-for-profit
  - Government-owned/operated
- Defining feature: **beds + infrastructure for inpatient care**.
- May also provide outpatient care.

### *Different Characteristics:*

1. **Level of services**
   - Basic → advanced → tertiary → quaternary care

2. **Focus**
   - **General hospital:** Broad range of patients/cases.
   - **Specialized hospital:** Focuses on a particular area.

3. **Teaching**
   - **Teaching hospital:** Trains new doctors.
   - **Academic hospital:** Strong association with a medical school.

---

## 2. Hospital & Physician Relationship

- **Hospital employs physician directly.**
- **Physician has separate practice** but an arrangement to practice at the hospital.

### *Admitting Privileges:*

- Arrangement allowing physicians to **provide services at a hospital**.
- Hospital and physician practice can remain **separate businesses** and be paid separately.

---

## 3. Hospital & Intermediaries

- Hospitals make arrangements with intermediaries to:
  - Care for covered patients.
  - Receive payment for that care.

### *Hospital Network:*

- Group of hospitals that an insurer works with.
- Enrollees may be required or encouraged to use them.

### *In-network:*

- Hospital that has a contract with an insurance company.

---

# Hospital Payment

## 1. Fee-for-Service (FFS)

- Payment is made for **each service provided**.

### *Chargemaster:*

- List of hospital services and the amount charged for each.

### *Cost-based reimbursement:*

- Hospital presents its costs to the intermediary.
- Payment is based on the **cost of care**.

---

## 2. Per Diem

- **Payment per patient day.**
- Fixed amount paid for each day a patient stays.
- Can vary by:
  - Type/complexity of services.
  - Day of hospital stay.
  - Carved-out services.

### *Carve-out:*

- Specific services paid **separately** from the per-diem.

---

## 3. DRG System

### *DRG = Diagnosis-Related Groups*

- Common payment method, generally for **inpatient care**.
- Hospital receives a **fixed payment per hospital stay** based on diagnosis.

### *How it works:*

**Diagnosis → DRG Grouper → DRG → Fixed Payment**

### *DRG Weight:*

- Value assigned to a DRG reflecting its **complexity/expected cost**.

### *DRG Incentives:*

- Payment is based on **patient characteristics**, not length of stay or services performed.

### *Outlier Payment:*

- Extra payment for patients requiring **much more care than expected**.

### *Prospective Payment:*

- Payment based on the **patient's condition at the outset**, not the actual treatments provided.

---

## 4. Global Budgets

### *Definition:*

- Hospital receives a **fixed amount for a specific period**, often one year.

Based on:

1. Known/predicted population.
2. Defined scope of services.

- Works best when it covers a **large share of the hospital's business**.
- Harder to use when many intermediaries make separate deals with hospitals.

---

# Inpatient vs Outpatient Payment

### *Inpatient:*

- Fee-for-service
- Per diem
- DRG

### *Outpatient:*

- More likely **fee-for-service**.
- Examples:
  - Lab services
  - X-rays
  - ER visits
  - Outpatient clinics

---

# Professional vs Facility Components

### *Professional Component:*

- Payment for the **physician/provider** providing the service.

### *Facility Component:*

- Payment for the **facility, personnel, infrastructure**, etc.

### *In Hospitals:*

- Professional and facility fees are generally **billed separately**.
- DRG covers the **facility part**.

### *Example — Surgery:*

- Surgeon bill
- Anesthesiologist bill
- Facility bill

---

# Charges vs Payments

- **Amount charged ≠ amount actually paid.**
- Hospitals may send detailed bills even when payment is based on per diem or DRG.
- Even under fee-for-service, there can be **discounts from the charged amount**.

---

# Risk & Incentives

### *Risk transferred to hospital:*

**Fee-for-Service → Per Diem → DRG → Global Budget**

- FFS → **Least risk**
- Per diem → **Some risk**
- DRG → **More risk**
- Global budget → **Most risk**

### *Incentives:*

- **FFS:** Incentive to provide **more services** → risk of overuse.
- **Per diem:** Incentive to keep patients **longer** → risk of overuse.
- **DRG:** Incentive to keep patients **shorter** → risk of underuse.
- **Global budget:** Strongest incentive to minimize **time and amount of care**.

### *Key Trade-off:*

- FFS / Per diem → less concern about **underuse**, but greater concern about **overuse**.
- DRG / Global budget → less concern about overuse, but greater risk of **underuse**.

# Larger Provider Organizations

## *Hospital Systems:*

- Multiple hospitals belonging to the **same company/organization**.

## *Physician-Hospital Organizations (PHOs):*

- Hospitals and physicians become part of the **same organization**.
- Example:
  - Hospital hires physicians.
  - Hospital buys physician practices.

## *Integrated Delivery Network (IDN):*

- Organization that owns or closely integrates **many types of providers**.
- Goal: provide a broad range of **coordinated care**.

---

# Financial & Clinical Integration

### *Financial Integration:*

- Providers work as part of the **same business**.
- Have a **unified bottom line**.

### *Clinical Integration:*

- Providers work together to provide **integrated and coordinated patient care**.

---

# Accountable Care Organization (ACO)

- Creates **clinical integration** across many providers.
- Providers can remain **separate organizations**.
- Originally designed for the **US Medicare program**.
- Providers voluntarily associate through contractual arrangements.
- Goal: **better, more coordinated care**.
- Can use payment arrangements such as **capitation** to encourage efficient care.

---

# Pay-for-Performance (P4P)

### *Definition:*

- Financial **incentives or penalties** based on whether providers meet predetermined performance expectations.

### *Measures:*

- **Clinical process:** Following guidelines.
- **Clinical outcomes:** Actual patient outcomes.
- **Patient experience:** Patient surveys.

- Often provided as a **performance-based bonus**.

### *Key Design Questions:*

- What measures should be used?
- What goal should be achieved?
- Should patient characteristics be adjusted for?
- How much money should be at stake?

---

# Electronic Records

## *EMR — Electronic Medical Record:*

- Electronic version of a patient's medical record.
- Usually within a particular practice/setting.

## *EHR — Electronic Health Record:*

- Can combine data from **multiple practices** into a more comprehensive record.

## *PHR — Personal Health Record:*

- Electronic application where **patients record their own health information**.
- Can be shared with providers.

---

# AI & Data in Healthcare

## *Payment & Innovation:*

### Fee-for-Service Organizations

- May adopt innovations if they can **bill and get paid** for them.
- May resist innovations that reduce services/revenue.

### Risk-Based Organizations

Examples:
- DRG
- Capitation

- More motivated to find **cost-reducing innovations**.
- Less interested in innovations that increase resource use.

## *Organization Size:*

- **Larger organizations:** More resources for IT and technology.
- **Smaller organizations:** May have fewer resources to adopt new tools.

## *Healthcare Data:*

- **EMR/EHR:** Useful data sources, but may not capture care across multiple organizations.
- **Hospital payment data:** Can capture information from multiple providers.
- Hospital bills can contain:
  - Diagnoses
  - Procedures
  - Related information
