## 1. What is Decision-Making Style?

Decision-making style is a person's usual way of **processing information and making choices**.

### Five Main Styles (GDMS)

- **Systematic:** Uses logic, facts, and careful analysis.
- **Intuitive:** Relies on feelings, instincts, or gut feeling.
- **Dependent:** Seeks advice, reassurance, or guidance from others.
- **Avoidant:** Delays, postpones, or avoids making decisions.
- **Spontaneous:** Makes decisions quickly, sometimes impulsively.

> People can use different styles depending on the situation. No style is always good or bad.

---

## 2. Childhood Experiences and Decision-Making

Childhood experiences can influence the development of decision-making abilities.

- **Childhood trauma and chronic stress** can affect brain development.
- Important brain regions include:
  - **Prefrontal cortex:** Planning, reasoning, impulse control, and decision-making.
  - **Amygdala:** Processes emotions and threats.
  - **Hippocampus:** Important for learning and memory.
- Effects depend on the **type, timing, intensity, and duration** of stressful experiences.
- Repeated or prolonged trauma can have stronger effects than isolated stressful events.

### Response Inhibition

**Response inhibition** is the ability to stop or control an inappropriate response.

Childhood adversity may be associated with weaker response inhibition, which can contribute to:

- Impulsive behavior
- Poor emotional control
- Difficulty making decisions under stress

---

## 3. Sibling Aggression

Sibling aggression includes repeated behaviors such as:

- Fighting
- Threats
- Intimidation
- Hostility
- Attempts to dominate another sibling

Research suggests that sibling aggression used to **establish or maintain dominance** can be associated with an **avoidant decision-making style** later in life.

### Possible Explanation

Repeated conflict may teach a child that:

- Conflict is threatening.
- Avoidance reduces immediate stress.
- Delaying decisions can feel safer.

Sibling aggression has also been associated with later:

- Anxiety
- Depression
- Aggressive behavior in relationships

> The motivation behind aggression may matter, not just whether someone was the aggressor or victim.

## 4. Neurobiological and Developmental Factors

### Prefrontal Cortex

The prefrontal cortex is involved in:

- Planning
- Reasoning
- Impulse control
- Evaluating consequences
- Complex decision-making

It continues developing into **early adulthood**, making childhood and adolescence important developmental periods.

### HPA Axis

The **hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis** controls the body's response to stress.

```text
Stress -> HPA Axis Activation -> Cortisol Release -> Stress Response
````

Chronic childhood stress can disrupt this system and potentially affect:

- Emotional regulation
- Attention
- Memory
- Brain connectivity
- Decision-making

---

## 5. Decision-Making Style vs Risk-Taking

**Decision-making style is not the same as risk-taking.**

For example:

- An avoidant person may delay decisions.
- A spontaneous person may decide quickly.
- Neither style automatically means the person takes more or less risk.

Childhood adversity may influence **how decisions are approached** without necessarily determining **how risky those decisions are**.

---

## 6. Key Takeaways

- Decision-making styles describe habitual ways of making choices.
- The GDMS identifies five styles: **systematic, intuitive, dependent, avoidant, and spontaneous**.
- People can use multiple styles depending on the situation.
- Childhood trauma and chronic stress can affect brain systems involved in decision-making.
- The **prefrontal cortex, amygdala, hippocampus, and HPA axis** are important in these processes.
- Sibling aggression, particularly dominance-related aggression, has been linked with later avoidant decision-making.
- Childhood experiences can influence decision-making, but they **do not permanently determine** a person's behavior.
